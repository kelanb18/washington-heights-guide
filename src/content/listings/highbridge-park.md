## What it is

Highbridge Park is a long, narrow park on the Harlem River side of Washington Heights. NYC Parks says it has 103 acres of forested natural area and is 2.25 miles long, running from W 155th St to Dyckman St between Edgecombe and Amsterdam Aves. The park is named for the High Bridge, the city's oldest standing bridge. NYC Parks says it was assembled piecemeal between 1867 and the 1960s.

## Who it's for

Walkers, cyclists, and anyone who wants river views and quiet away from Broadway. NYC Parks describes open vistas, an unusual geology of cliffs and rock outcroppings, the city's first mountain-biking course, and a skate park. The Highbridge Recreation Center and Pool, built in 1936 on the site of the former reservoir, is inside the park.

## Standout details

The park holds the High Bridge and the Highbridge Water Tower, and its northern end is Fort George Hill, where an amusement park with Ferris wheels and roller coasters stood from 1895 until a fire in 1913. NYC Parks also reports a 2021 reconstruction that added a renovated adventure playground, a fitness area, and a new turf soccer field, and says the park supports hawks, many bird species, and a rediscovered population of dusky salamanders.

## Honest verdict

A big, hilly park with real natural character and fewer visitors than Fort Tryon, which also means less polish. Not every section is the same: the tower, bridge, and playgrounds are maintained, while the long wooded stretches are steep. Check NYC Parks for current closures, since the recreation center posts schedule changes on its page.
