Highbridge Park is a narrow band of woods, cliffs, and ball fields that runs 2.25 miles along the Harlem River, according to NYC Parks. It sits below the avenues on the Fort George and Jumel Terrace sides of Washington Heights, so it overlaps with those areas on the map, but it has its own landmarks and its own way in: the High Bridge to the Bronx, the Highbridge Water Tower, the Highbridge Pool and Recreation Center, and mountain-bike trails.

NYC Parks says the park was assembled piecemeal between 1867 and the 1960s. The northern section, Fort George Hill, covers the site of the old Fort George and of the Fort George amusement park that opened in 1895 and burned in 1913. NYC Parks says it acquired the cliffside from W 181st St to Dyckman St in 1902. At the southern end, Coogan's Bluff is the escarpment that runs roughly from W 155th to W 160th St between Edgecombe Ave and the river, according to Wikipedia; the Polo Grounds, the Giants' ballpark until 1957, stood in the bottomland below it until 1964.
