Hudson Heights sits on the high ridge that overlooks the Hudson River on one side and the Broadway valley on the other. Bennett Park, at W 183rd to 185th Sts between Pinehurst and Fort Washington Aves, holds the highest natural point in Manhattan, 265 feet above sea level. The neighborhood is known for its 1920s and 1930s apartment buildings, many of them Art Deco, and for being a quieter, more residential pocket than the avenues below.

The name is new. Wikipedia's Washington Heights article says it was created in 1992 by the Hudson Heights Owners' Coalition to promote co-op sales in the northwest part of the neighborhood, and that some residents and historians dislike it because it separates the area from the rest of Washington Heights. Treat the boundaries as informal: the narrow definition stops at the ridge (Fort Washington Ave on the east, W 181st St on the south), while a wider one runs down to W 173rd St and across to Broadway.

The main north-south streets are Fort Washington Ave (two-way), Pinehurst Ave (one-way south), and Cabrini Blvd (one-way north) along the western edge. The A train stops at W 181st St and W 190th St, and both stations have elevators that lift riders from the Broadway valley up to the ridge. Fort Tryon Park and The Cloisters anchor the northern end. Day-to-day shopping and eating cluster in two small strips, W 181st St near Fort Washington Ave and W 187th St, covered in the two sub-areas below.
